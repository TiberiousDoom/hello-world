Foundry Front · art proposal · nests

Nests that grow, not just get bigger

Today a nest is one burrow sprite drawn 1.7, 2 or 2.3 tiles across by stage, with an egg-clutch overlay added at stages 2 and 3. These three directions draw each stage as its own picture, so a stage 3 nest has new parts a stage 1 nest doesn’t.

Each direction covers all three kinds and a queen. They are concepts drawn in code, to choose a direction from; the chosen one gets a proper polish pass before it goes in the game.

Try the preview: it puts one nest on grass with its blight, at game scale, with the 2×2 tiles it blocks marked.

Today’s nests, for comparison

A · Earthworks

The nest digs outward. It starts as one hole in a low mound and becomes a dig site: side burrows joined by raised tunnel ridges, spoil heaps, egg clutches, and by stage 3 tunnels leaving the mound and scrap stolen from your factory.

Stage by stage

  1. One burrow in a low mound, a few clods.
  2. A wider mound, a second burrow joined by a tunnel ridge, the first eggs.
  3. Five burrows, tunnels heading off the mound, spoil, scrap, two clutches.

Kinds

Warrens: shed crawler husks. Hives: paper comb collars instead of soil rims. Dens: boulders, claw gouges, bones.

How it would sit in the game

Closest to today’s art, so the least risky. Reads well from above. Growth shows as more holes, which also hints “more bugs”.

B · Brood

The nest is alive. A single glossy egg pod becomes a cluster of pods with veins creeping into the ground, then a ribbed chitin dome with a glowing maw, ringed by egg sacs, its roots reaching the edge of the blight.

Stage by stage

  1. One pod half sunk in turned soil, short veins.
  2. A cluster of four pods round an open one, longer veins, eggs.
  3. A shelled dome like a bug’s back, a ring of seven sacs, thick roots.

Kinds

Each kind takes its bugs’ colours: crawler orange, runner navy with wasp-yellow sacs, brute crimson with bone spikes.

How it would sit in the game

The most readable kind at a glance (colour) and the biggest change from stage to stage. Gives the veins a job later: they could pulse while angry. Moves the look away from “holes in the ground”.

C · Spires

The bugs build upward, like termite mounds. A lump with a hole becomes a chimney with a younger one beside it, then a cluster of towers round a tall central spire, each with vents and a long shadow.

Stage by stage

  1. A low cone on a patch of dug soil.
  2. A tall chimney with a smaller one behind, side vents.
  3. Five towers round a spire half again as tall, scrap and eggs at the foot.

Kinds

Warrens: ribbed mud. Hives: banded grey paper with comb at the foot. Dens: stacked stone with red crust and bones.

How it would sit in the game

The most striking silhouette, and height makes a big nest feel dangerous from far off. But it is the only direction drawn with height, so it leans 3/4 where most of the game is straight down, and tall towers cover the tiles behind them.

What it takes to put one in

  • Sprites: 9 per direction (nests/warren_1 to nests/den_3) plus nests/queen. Hardcore gets a darker take on each stage instead of one nests/hardcore sprite.
  • Size: every stage drawn in the same 4-tile box (the Hardcore cover ring is 4×4 too), and the art itself grows: about 1.6 tiles across at stage 1, 2.5 at stage 2, 3.4 at stage 3. The blocked 2×2 tiles don’t change.
  • Merged nests: stage 3 art at 1.3× as now; for A and B, a tunnel or vein drawn in code towards each other cell the nest owns.
  • Kept as they are: the angry eyes, damaged rim and destroyed overlays, the health bar and the blight.
  • Optional, later: a short crossfade when a nest moves up a stage, and for B, veins that pulse while angry.
  • Code: one change in Renderer.drawNest (pick the sprite by stage, drop the nests/grown overlay), TILES in art/index.ts, ART_LIST.md, and a decision row. No change to the rules or saves.
